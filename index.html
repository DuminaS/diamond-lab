How the combine grade works

Combine Grade and Football OVR are deliberately different. Combine Grade measures the completeness of all twelve picks. Football OVR uses the same position-specific weights as the career simulation, so accuracy, pocket play, anticipation, decision-making, and clutch matter more than secondary traits. Football OVR is the number teams use for your draft projection.

Your grade starts as the average of your twelve picks. Big gaps between your best and weakest pick cost you — the lab rewards a complete quarterback, not one huge number and eleven shrugs. Keep every pick at 90+ and you earn a small floor bonus.

The ceiling sits well under 100 on purpose: almost none of the 200+ quarterbacks in the pool ever grade above the low-to-mid 90s in any single skill, and nobody grades that high everywhere. A score in the 70s from an all-decade build is genuinely great.

The pool blends 80 real passers from NFL history with a larger set of generated prospects, so there's real depth beyond the household names. Every generated player is built from a transparent formula, not a gut call: a tier (Elite down to Camp Arm) sets a target overall, an archetype (Pocket Passer, Gunslinger, Dual-Threat, Game Manager, Field General) decides which of the twelve ratings sit above or below that target, and a bit of jitter keeps no two prospects identical. That's why a 64 is a 64 and not a 70 — it's math, not vibes.

How your build shapes your career

Every season, your twelve ratings are aged for how old you are that year (arm and legs peak in your mid-20s and fade after 30; touch and decision-making keep improving into your 30s) and then reweighted for the decade you're playing in — mobility and durability matter far more in the leather-tough 1960s-70s, while touch and anticipation matter far more in the rule-friendly 2010s-20s. The same build plays like a different player depending which era you enter.

Short/intermediate accuracy, touch, deep accuracy, and anticipation drive completion percentage. Arm strength, deep accuracy, touch, and improvisation drive yards per attempt. Anticipation, decision-making, and touch drive touchdowns; decision-making, anticipation, and pocket presence hold down interceptions. Mobility and improvisation now also generate real rushing production — a scrambler build racks up rush yards and touchdowns on top of the passing line, not just a passing-game penalty for everyone else. Durability drives your injury odds, and a blended overall rating drives team record, awards, contract tier, and whether you keep your job. The gap between a backup-caliber build and an elite one is deliberately wide — expect very different stat lines, not the same box score with a different name on it.

Your team has its own grade, separate from you, and it moves on its own from year to year — coaching changes, roster churn, a good or bad draft class — the same way a real front office can hand a great quarterback a bad supporting cast (or vice versa). Your rating does not magically improve the defense, coaching staff, or front office: a legendary arm can still lose games on a team that's falling apart around him.

Standings are now a real league: 8 divisions across 2 conferences, with every other team simulated off its own evolving grade. Division winners plus the best remaining records seed a 7-team playoff bracket per conference, so your seed and your record can never contradict each other — check the "View league standings" button on any season card to see the full picture. Only a Super Bowl appearance gets the quarter-by-quarter simulation and box score; every other round just shows the final.

That overall rating is also what a team is paying for. Play well and free agency brings multiple offers — a hometown deal, a contender, or the biggest paycheck on the board. Decline, and the options shrink to a prove-it minimum, or nothing at all. Aging veterans on bad teams get traded; quarterbacks who fall off a cliff get waived outright, with only a slim chance of catching on elsewhere. Very few careers make it anywhere near 40.
